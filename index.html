Where the six values come from

The six core values are not extra content. They are dispositions that the content you already teach can develop, if the activity asks students to act on them and the assessment is built to catch them.

The three learning domains

A value is not tied to one domain; the level and context in which it is taught decide where it lives (GVBE, Item 78). MQA's own example maps Justice to all three.

Cognitive, the head

"Explain how legal frameworks and social systems can promote or hinder justice."

Evidence: written analysis, case analysis, ethical argument.

Affective, the heart

"Demonstrate respect for diverse perspectives and a willingness to challenge injustice."

Evidence: reflective accounts, observation, peer and partner feedback.

Psychomotor, the hands

"Perform a negotiation or mediation role-play that shows ethical and just conduct."

Evidence: role-play performance, service outputs, decisions taken.

A written exam reaches only the head. It can show whether a student can explain a value, not whether they act on it. The affective domain is dominant in VBE (GVBE, Item 106), so most value outcomes need a task that produces affective or behavioural evidence. Choose the output first; the rubric follows.

The UPM VBE framework: six components

VBE at UPM is a key mechanism for translating the UPM Educational Philosophy, the UPM Educational Goals, the UPM core values and the UPM graduate attributes systematically into the curriculum of academic programmes.

Six components surround the Berilmu Berbakti core; the four JIWA graduate attributes sit at the corners. Each component is one way the Berilmu Berbakti aspiration enters the curriculum (CADe-Lead briefing, slides 14 to 15).

  1. Purpose and educational outcomes Tujuan dan Hasil PendidikanValues stated clearly in PEO, PLO and CLO.
  2. Knowledge and research Ilmu dan PenyelidikanKnowledge integrated with real issues, ethics and sustainability, for the benefit of society.
  3. Pedagogy and learning experience Pedagogi dan Pengalaman PembelajaranMeaningful, collaborative, student-centred learning that builds critical thinking, empathy and social responsibility.
  4. Assessment and reflection Pentaksiran dan RefleksiValues made part of the culture through authentic assessment, reflective activities and continuous feedback.
  5. Technology and digital ethics Teknologi dan Etika DigitalTechnology used ethically, responsibly, inclusively and with integrity.
  6. Stakeholders and impact Pihak Berkepentingan dan ImpakLearning linked to community, industry and society through meaningful engagement and knowledge-based solutions.

Where the six MQF values sit: Berilmu and Berbakti

At UPM, the MQF core values must be mapped to the UPM core values, the graduate attributes and the needs of each discipline. They are the basis for choosing sub-values that suit the course, its T&L activities and its assessment (CADe-Lead briefing, slide 8; Table 3, slide 12).

Sub-values can grow. The MQF sub-values are guidance; values may be added to make them more inclusive (Table 2 note). UPM's own sub-values above are an example. Keep the sub-value in the same category as its core value.

Phased implementation

Approved by Senate 758 (13 August 2026), in line with MQF 2.0: from Infuse or Embed, to Embed, then Integrate for new and reviewed programmes (CADe-Lead briefing, slides 16 to 20).

Phase 1 · Semester 1, 2026/2027

Infuse or Embed

Every programme starts VBE through one of the two approaches in one selected course. Infuse is a starting phase, valid for two semesters only.

Phase 2 · From Semester 1, 2027/2028

Embed

Every programme implements VBE through Embed, following the UPM VBE implementation guideline to be set.

Phase 3 · New and reviewed programmes from 2028/2029

Integrate

Implement at Integrate level directly, without passing through Infuse or Embed.

Integrate involves: VBE in programme curriculum design · mapping values at programme level · aligning course learning outcomes · T&L activities and assessment methods.

The six core values

A definition tells you what to say in week one. An observable behaviour tells you what to look for when marking. Open each value for its meaning, its sub-values, its place in Berilmu or Berbakti, and the three behaviours that seed rubric criteria 1 to 3.

Six values, one per course

Built from the cardinal virtues and focus-group consultation, and aligned with the FPK and the Rukun Negara (GVBE, Item 57). They are developmental: a first-year student meets Justice as classroom fairness, a final-year student as systemic inequity or policy (Item 69).

Set your discipline

Pedagogy

Pedagogy is where values are activated, negotiated and experienced (GVBE, Item 88). The value stays the same whatever method you choose; the assessment must change to capture what the method produces.

Explicit and implicit teaching

Explicit

The value is named, discussed and reflected on: an ethical case, a guided dialogue, a structured reflection.

Implicit

The value is lived through the environment: how students are treated, how collaboration runs, how difference is respected. You are the role model.

The five strategies

MQA groups VBE pedagogy into five strategies, each suited to particular values (GVBE, Table 5.1). Read down the column of your value, not across the row of the strategy you like.

Three separate decisions. Method, value and assessment are chosen separately. A lively role-play still assessed by a recall exam has changed nothing.
Practice, not mention. Did every student act on the value at least twice, with feedback? One activity is an anecdote; two spaced activities with feedback build a capability.

The delivery methods menu

MQA groups delivery methods into five categories, from cognitive to immersive (GVBE, Figure 5.2 and Appendix 2B). Choose one you can run twice, and sequence from low-stakes, low-resource methods towards higher-stakes ones (Item 110).

Watch out. Discussion feels like learning and often is not. Every dialogic or relational method needs a short written output afterwards, or there is nothing to assess. Hours served is not evidence; neither is a partner's thanks.

Low-cost infusions

Five minutes, delivered orally: exactly what Infuse asks for at UPM. They will not carry an Embed claim alone, but they are how most lecturers start.

The five-minute nudge

After teaching why dam walls thicken with depth, ask: "Whose river is it? How do we balance energy, ecology and equity?" (GVBE, Example 1, Physics).

The snippet

One image or clip linking concept to consequence: why does the Möbius strip suit the recycling symbol? (GVBE, Item 109).

The cost clause

Add to any design task: what does this trade off, and who bears it?

The absent party

Who is affected by this calculation and was not consulted?

The honest limit

Add to any lab or data task: report one result that did not fit, and what it means.

The thank-you that names

Close a group task by naming one specific contribution from someone else.

Assessment and alignment

When a value is assessed, choose the output first; the rubric follows. At UPM, under Embed the value is stated in the LLO and carries marks, so it is assessed with a rubric and marked student work is kept as evidence. At Integrate it is assessed across the programme.

A knowledge question versus a values question

Assesses knowledge

"Explain the importance of integrity in engineering practice."

A student who memorised the code scores full marks. Nothing but length separates the levels.

Assesses the value

"The cheaper design meets the budget but not the safety margin. Your firm needs the contract. Recommend, justify by the code, and state what it costs the firm."

The student must act on the value to answer; each clause becomes a criterion.

Test every task the same way. Could a well-prepared student score highly without acting on the value? If yes, redesign before writing the rubric.

Rubric anatomy

Four criteria per value: three from the observable behaviours, plus a fourth for what the student does with the value. Four levels, using MQA's labels and aligned to Bloom's affective domain (GVBE, Tables 5.5 and 5.6). Write levels 1 and 4 first, then split the gap.

1EmergingReceiving

Notices the value: names it, uses respectful language, states an opinion

2DevelopingResponding

Responds to it: some reasons, one perspective beyond their own

3ProficientValuing

Acts on it with justification. The expected standard mid-programme

4TransformativeOrganising and characterising

Holds it consistently, even when it costs something or is challenged

Four faults to avoid

Adjectives"Excellent, good, fair" is a grading scale, not a descriptor.
Counting"Three perspectives" versus "one" measures volume, not quality.
Restating the brief"Well structured" assesses writing, not the value.
Written too lateA rubric students never saw is a marking aid, not a design.

The six rubrics

Criteria 1 to 3 come from the observable behaviours; criterion 4 is what the student does with the value. Adapt the wording to your discipline before use.

Writing the levels

Write level 1 from the commonest error, and level 4 from the exemplary move; then split the gap. Differentiate by quality, never by counting. In every value, the gradient reads:

asserts›reasons›applies a principle›withstands challenge

Fairness and moderation

Values marks attract more challenges, because students rightly see more judgement in them. Four safeguards, in order of value for effort.

Safeguard 1

Publish the rubric with the brief

Non-negotiable; it improves student work. Record the date.

Safeguard 2

Calibrate before marking

Two markers score the same three scripts, then reconcile. About thirty minutes.

Safeguard 3

Moderate after marking

A 10% sample plus all borderline cases. Record it; this is what an auditor asks for.

Safeguard 4

Keep annotated exemplars

One per level, anonymised. Teaching material next year; evidence if a mark is contested.

Thirty minutes. Calibration before marking prevents most of the disputes that would otherwise arrive after it.

Writing a values-explicit LLO

Under Embed, the chosen value or sub-value must be stated in the lesson learning outcome. Use an observable verb; avoid understand, appreciate, be aware of and know. The same stems serve CLOs at Integrate.

The test. Can you write four different rubric descriptors for this verb? If not, it is not observable, and a reviewer cannot trace it.

Two parts. Part A tests the design decisions from the Pedagogy and Assessment stages. Part B gives you ten tasks from your own cluster and asks which value each one assesses, and whether it assesses the value at all.

Ten tasks. For some, name the value the task primarily assesses. For others, judge whether the task can assess the named value at all. The one rule decides it: if a student could score full marks without acting on the value, it does not assess it.

Key take-aways

The principles to carry into your course file. Everything here serves one purpose: a value claim you could defend to a student, and to an auditor.

Seven things to carry out of this module

  1. Values are lived, not listed.VBE asks students to act on a value, not describe it. Knowing the code of ethics is Values-Education; keeping it when it costs something is Integrity.
  2. One value, one of its own sub-values.Choose one MQF core value and a sub-value from the same category, mapped to Berilmu or Berbakti. A course that claims six values evidences none of them.
  3. Know your approach.The programme decides. Infuse is oral, brief and changes no outcomes, and is allowed for two semesters only in Phase 1. Embed is for every programme from 2027/2028.
  4. Under Embed, the LLO is the gatekeeper.State the value in the lesson learning outcome with an observable verb. PEO, PLO and CLO stay unchanged.
  5. Change the task, not the content.Embedding a value rarely needs new topics. It needs a question with a real values tension, practised twice with feedback.
  6. Mark quality, not position or sentiment.Under Embed the value carries marks: four criteria and four levels, published with the brief and applied to individual evidence.
  7. Record it.Keep the LLO, activity sheet, rubric and samples of student work in the course file. A claim that cannot be followed to evidence is not an implementation.

A checklist before the semester starts

  • The programme's approach (Infuse or Embed) and the selected core course are confirmed.
  • One core value and one of its own sub-values, mapped to Berilmu or Berbakti.
  • The topic contains a real values tension.
  • Infuse: what you will say, and when, is planned. Nothing is written or assessed.
  • Embed: the value is stated in the LLO with an observable verb.
  • Two spaced practice activities with feedback.
  • Embed: the value carries marks, the task cannot be completed well without acting on it, and the rubric is published with the brief.
  • Programme Standard or professional body requirements are taken into account.
  • The VBE evidence record is set up in the course file. Under Embed it includes the marked samples of student work.
  • The course outline states the approach, the value and the UPM core value.
The one rule, one last time. If a student could score full marks without acting on the value, you have taught about the value, not through it.
