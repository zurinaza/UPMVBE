Stage 01 · Introduction

About this module

A working module for making one MQF core value teachable, observable and assessable inside a course you already teach. It follows the MQA Guidelines on Values-Based Education (2026) and UPM's guidebook for lecturers.

If a student could score full marks without acting on the value, you have taught about the value, not through it.

The one rule. Every design decision in this module follows from it.

1.1 Three objectives for this module

1

Understand the six

Know what Dignity, Justice, Integrity, Gratitude, Courage and Altruism mean, and what each looks like in student work.

2

Design for practice

Plan activities in which students act on one value, at least twice, with feedback in between.

3

Assess it defensibly

Write a task and a four-criterion rubric that an auditor can follow from the value to a marked script.

1.2 What you leave with

Seven outputs, all for one course: yours. Stage 09 builds them with you and keeps them on this device.

Two practice activities with feedback, placed in your semester

A four-criterion, four-level rubric, ready to publish with the brief
An alignment table and the wording for your course outline
A review plan for the next semester

Stage 02 · The framework

How the parts fit together

What separates values-based education from teaching about values, why it is required, the three domains that decide what a task can show, UPM's three tiers, and the chain an auditor follows from a value to a marked script.

2.1 Teaching about values, or living through them

MQA draws a firm line here. Values-Education teaches about values; Values-Based Education requires students to live through them (GVBE, Item 6). The MQA briefing deck calls it the shift from teaching to living.

AspectValues-Education (VE)Values-Based Education (VBE)
FocusTeaching about valuesLiving through values
ApproachCognitive and theoreticalHolistic: emotional, spiritual and social
ImplementationOften a separate subjectIntegrated into ethos, curriculum and relationships
GoalUnderstand moral conceptsDevelop character and embed values in daily behaviour
AssessmentMainly knowledge-basedReflection, behavioural observation, community feedback

Source: GVBE, Table 1.1.

2.2 The mandate

This is a national requirement, not a local initiative. Three instruments stand behind it.

Education Act 1996

The National Education Philosophy (FPK) seeks individuals who are "intellectually, spiritually, emotionally and physically balanced and harmonious", with "high moral standards" (GVBE, Item 22).

MQF Second Edition, 2024

Renewed emphasis on infusing, embedding and integrating values across all qualification levels, through the five clusters of learning outcomes (GVBE, Item 23).

GVBE 2026, Item 24

Higher education providers must set institutional VBE guidelines covering core values, approach, delivery, resources, staff training, and monitoring and accountability.

Read the modal verbs. Must is mandatory and binding. Should is expected unless a departure is justified with an equivalent alternative. Could is good practice and counts as evidence of continuous improvement.

MQF 2024 also asks that values and sustainability competencies are not run as separate agendas (GVBE, Item 68). One task can carry a core value and an ESD competency at the same time; the companion ESD module covers the competency side.

2.3 The three learning domains

A value is not tied to one domain. The level and context in which it is taught decide where it lives (GVBE, Item 78). MQA's own example places Justice in all three.

Cognitive, the head

Concepts and reasoning about the value.

Explain how legal frameworks and social systems can promote or hinder justice.

Evidence: written analysis, case analysis, ethical argument.

Affective, the heart

Attitudes, empathy and moral commitment.

Show respect for diverse perspectives and a willingness to challenge injustice.

Evidence: reflective accounts, observation, peer and partner feedback.

Psychomotor, the hands

Acting on the value in a real or simulated setting.

Perform a negotiation or mediation role-play showing ethical and just conduct.

Evidence: role-play performance, service outputs, decisions taken.

A written exam reaches only the head. It can test whether a student can explain a value. It cannot show whether they act on it. The affective domain is the dominant domain in VBE (GVBE, Item 106), so most value outcomes need a task that produces affective or behavioural evidence. Choose the output first; the rubric follows.

2.4 UPM's three tiers

The tier says how deep the value goes in your course. MQA expects providers to start at Infuse and move towards Embed and Integrate as capacity grows (GVBE, Items 24 and 82).

InfusePenerapan Asas

A brief, often implicit mention of a value in a session: a prompt, a snippet, a five-minute nudge.

Not assessed: awareness only
EmbedPenerapan Terancang

The value is placed on purpose in a module, activity or assessment: a value-explicit outcome, an activity and a rubric.

Assessed within the course
IntegratePengintegrasian Menyeluruh

Systemic alignment across curriculum, pedagogy, assessment and culture: CLO-to-PLO mapping, progression across semesters, co-curricular links.

Assessed across the programme

Source: GVBE, Table 4.3.

Infuse is a starting point, not an end point. MQA calls it the minimal level, suitable as a short-term measure, and "not sufficient on its own to sustain meaningful change" (GVBE, Item 82). Count Infuse items as awareness. Do not report them as value attainment.

2.5 The rules for a course

UPM adopts the six MQF core values and their sub-values as MQA publishes them, without UPM-specific additions.

Sub-value per courseOne, from that value's MQF sub-value list
TierDeclared as Infuse, Embed or Integrate in the course outline
AssessmentEmbed and Integrate only: one task producing individual evidence

2.6 What an auditor follows, left to right

A claim that cannot be followed along this line is a claim, not an outcome.

ValueCore value and sub-value, named in the outline and checklist
→
OutcomeAn observable verb, at lesson, assessment or CLO level
→
CriterionOne of four, with four level descriptors
→
ScriptIndividual evidence, marked and annotated
→
AttainmentComputed from those criteria only

Three things break the chain: an outcome that is not mapped, a criterion that measures writing quality instead of the value, and a group report with no individual output.

2.7 The workflow at a glance

Eight steps, from MQA's recommended actions for designing VBE learning (GVBE, Item 112). Start from the content and outcome you already have, never from the list of values. Stage 09 walks you through them.

  1. Clarify the whyConfirm the outcome, name the value and sub-value, align with PEO, PLO, CLO and mission.
  2. Choose the scopeInfuse, Embed or Integrate; lesson, course or programme.
  3. Select the strategyExperiential, reflective, relational, participatory or integrative.
  4. Match to contextDiscipline, readiness, class size, spaces, technology, your role.
  5. Design the flowLow stakes to high; practise the value twice with feedback.
  6. Select methodsThe method that best activates the strategy and outcome.
  7. Align assessmentOne task, individual evidence; four criteria, four levels, published early.
  8. Review and refineFeedback from students, peers and partners; adjust delivery and assessment.

If you cannot complete a step, the problem is usually upstream: content with no real tension in it (step 1), or a value the content cannot evidence (step 2).

Stage 03 · Understand

The six core values

A definition tells you what to say in week one. An observable behaviour tells you what to look for when marking. Open each value for its meaning, its MQF sub-values, and the three behaviours that seed rubric criteria 1 to 3.

3.1 Six values, one per course

Built from the cardinal virtues and focus-group consultation, and aligned with the FPK and the Rukun Negara (GVBE, Item 57). They are developmental: a first-year student meets Justice as classroom fairness; a final-year student meets it as systemic inequity or policy (GVBE, Item 69).

A note on Courage. The MQA briefing deck (2025) labels the fifth value "Resilience". The GVBE (2026) names it Courage, with Resilience as its first sub-value (Table 3.2). This module follows the GVBE.

3.2 Four distinctions that prevent mis-selection

Most mis-selection happens between these pairs. If both halves of a pair describe your task equally well, the task is not yet sharp enough to carry a value claim.

The line to carry into every later stage. If a student could score full marks without acting on the value, you have not assessed it, whatever your course outline claims.

3.3 Before you move on

You are ready for your own discipline when you can:

  • State the shift each value demands of student work.
  • Say which observable behaviour separates strong work from merely polite or confident work.
  • Tell apart Integrity and Justice, Dignity and Altruism, Gratitude and Altruism, and Courage and confidence.
  • State the one rule in your own words.

Stage 06 · Pedagogy

Mentioning a value is not teaching it.

Pedagogy is where values are activated, negotiated and experienced (GVBE, Item 88). This stage gives MQA's five strategies and five method families, the five-minute infusions most lecturers start with, and one assessment redesigned with almost no new content.

6.1 Three separate decisions

The method

Creates the experience in which students act on the value.

The value

Stays the same whichever method you choose.

The assessment

Must change to capture what the method actually produces.

A lively role-play that is still assessed by a recall exam has changed nothing. Usually only the first two decisions get made.

6.2 Explicit and implicit teaching

Values are learned two ways at once, and both count (GVBE, Item 88).

Explicit

The value is named, discussed and reflected on: an ethical case, a guided dialogue, a structured reflection.

Implicit

The value is lived through the learning environment: how students are treated, how collaboration runs, how difference is respected. This hidden curriculum shapes attitudes as strongly as instruction, which is why you are a role model as well as a facilitator.

6.3 Practice, not mention

The test. Did every student do something, at least twice, that required them to act on the value, and receive feedback on it? One activity produces an anecdote. Two spaced activities with feedback produce a capability.

6.4 The five strategies

MQA groups VBE pedagogy into five strategies (GVBE, Table 5.1 and Appendix 1). Pick the one that suits your value and your content.

6.5 The delivery methods

Five method families (GVBE, Figure 5.2 and Appendix 2B). Choose one method you could run twice in the semester, for example in week 5 and again in week 9, with feedback in between.

Watch out. Discussion feels like learning and often is not. Every dialogic or relational method needs a short written output afterwards, or there is nothing to assess.

6.6 Five-minute infusions

These will not carry an Embed claim on their own, but they are how most lecturers start, and they make the later claim easier.

The five-minute nudge

MQA's Physics example: after showing that dam walls are thicker at the base because pressure rises with depth, ask "Whose river is it? How do we balance energy, ecology and equity?" (GVBE, Appendix 3, Example 1).

The snippet

One image or short clip that links the concept to a human consequence. MQA's mathematics example asks why the Möbius strip suits the recycling symbol (GVBE, Item 109).

The cost clause

Add to any design task: what does this trade off, and who bears it?

The absent party

Who is affected by this calculation and was not consulted?

The honest limit

Add to any lab or data task: report one result that did not fit, and what it means.

The thank-you that names

Close a group task by naming one specific contribution from someone else.

6.7 One assessment, redesigned

MQA's Geotechnical Engineering example (MQF Level 6, Semester 6) shows Embed with almost no new content (GVBE, Appendix 3, Example 3).

Before

  • Lectures on slope stability.
  • Analyse the failure; propose a retaining wall and soil nailing (40 marks).
  • Estimate costs from the JKR Schedule of Rates (40 marks).
  • No decision required, no pressure.
  • Value evidenced: none.

After: Integrity embedded

  • Same lectures; integrity raised in discussion.
  • Same analysis and costing (80 marks).
  • Recommend a solution using the Engineering Code of Ethics (6 marks).
  • Recommend again when the cheaper choice would cost four staff their jobs (14 marks).
  • Value evidenced: Integrity, individually justified.

Added cost: two questions and one short rubric. The analysis does not change; the student must now defend a decision that has no single right answer.

Stage 07 · Assess

Assessment tasks and rubrics

Choose the output first; the rubric follows. A rubric written over the wrong task cannot be repaired by better descriptors. MQA asks that assessment be valid, fair, transparent and developmentally appropriate, and that it measure ethical judgement, not only knowledge (GVBE, Item 95).

7.1 A knowledge question versus a values question

Assesses knowledge

"Explain the importance of integrity in engineering practice."

A student who memorised the code of ethics scores full marks. Nothing but length separates the levels.

Assesses the value

"The cheaper design meets the client's budget but not the safety margin in the code. Your firm needs the contract. Recommend, justify by the code, and state what your recommendation costs the firm."

The student must act on the value to answer, and each clause becomes a criterion.

Test every task the same way: could a well-prepared student score highly without acting on the value? If yes, redesign before writing the rubric.

7.5 Anatomy of a usable rubric

Four criteria per value: three from the observable behaviours, plus a fourth for what the student does with the value. Four levels with MQA's labels (GVBE, Table 5.5), aligned to Bloom's affective domain (Table 5.6). Descriptors differ by quality, never by counting.

1 · EmergingReceivingNotices the value: names it, uses respectful language, states an opinion.
2 · DevelopingRespondingResponds to it: some reasons, one perspective beyond their own.
3 · ProficientValuingActs on it with justification; the expected standard mid-programme.
4 · TransformativeOrganising and characterisingHolds it consistently, including when it costs something or is challenged.
The technique. Write levels 1 and 4 first, then split the gap. Writing levels in order produces a flat, undifferentiated middle.

7.6 The four faults

Adjectives, not descriptors

"Excellent, good, fair" is a grading scale. It tells the student nothing about what to do.

Levels separated by counting

"Three perspectives" versus "one" measures volume, not the value.

Criteria that restate the brief

"Well structured" assesses report writing, not the value.

Written after the task

A rubric students never saw is a marking aid, not an assessment design.

7.7 The six rubrics

Criteria 1 to 3 come from the observable behaviours; criterion 4 is what the student does with the value. Adapt the wording to your discipline before use.

7.8 Three traps when marking

Marking the position

Rewarding students whose values match yours. Fix: mark whether the principle was applied consistently and the objection met.

Marking sincerity

Grading reflection on how heartfelt it sounds. Fix: mark specificity and reasoning.

Marking the group

Using a group report as individual evidence. Fix: attach a short individual output: a reflection, revision log or justified peer assessment.

7.9 Fairness and moderation

Values marks attract more challenges, because students rightly see more judgement in them. Four safeguards, in order of value for effort.

  1. Publish firstRelease the rubric with the brief and record the date.
  2. CalibrateTwo markers score the same three scripts before marking, about thirty minutes.
  3. ModerateSecond-mark a 10% sample plus all borderline cases.
  4. Keep exemplarsOne annotated, anonymised script per level.

7.10 Writing an assessable outcome

Only the outcome is directly assessed; every other column in the alignment table is a claim traceable through it. MQA shows a value travelling down the outcome hierarchy (GVBE, Items 73 to 74).

PEOGraduates "embody and champion values-based leadership and ethical citizenship".
→
PLO"Engage in selfless service and collaborative action that prioritises collective well-being (Altruism)."
→
CLO"Make decisions with integrity" (Geotechnical Engineering, Appendix 3, Example 3).

Use an observable verb. Avoid understand, appreciate, be aware of and know: none of them can be seen in student work.

The test. Can you write four different rubric descriptors for this verb? If not, it is not assessable.

Stage 10 · Summary

Key take-aways

The principles to carry into your course file. Everything here serves one purpose: a value claim you could defend to a student, and to an auditor.

10.1 Seven things to carry out of this module

  1. Values are lived, not listed.VBE asks students to act on a value, not describe it. Knowing the code of ethics is Values-Education; keeping it when it costs something is Integrity.
  2. Change the task, not the content.Embedding a value rarely needs new topics. It needs a question with a real values tension. MQA's Geotechnical redesign added two questions and one short rubric.
  3. One value, one sub-value, one tier.Depth beats coverage. A course that claims six values evidences none of them.
  4. The outcome is the gatekeeper.Only the outcome is directly assessed. Write it with a verb you could build four rubric levels from.
  5. Practise twice, with feedback.One activity produces an anecdote. Two spaced activities with feedback produce a capability.
  6. Mark quality, not position or sentiment.Four criteria and four levels, published with the brief, applied to individual evidence.
  7. Infuse is where you start, not where you stop.Count Infuse as awareness, never as attainment, and move towards Embed and Integrate as capacity grows.

10.2 A design checklist before you publish

  • The topic contains a real values tension.
  • One core value and one sub-value from the MQF list, recorded in the Senarai Semak VBE.
  • The tier is declared in the course outline.
  • A value-explicit outcome with an observable verb, mapped to an MQF domain.
  • Two spaced practice activities with feedback.
  • The task cannot be completed well without acting on the value.
  • Individual evidence wherever there is group work.
  • Four criteria and four levels, published with the task brief.
  • Calibration planned before marking, moderation after.
  • Self-ratings and surveys used formatively, never as the mark.
The one rule, one last time. If a student could score full marks without acting on the value, you have not assessed it, whatever your outline claims. Apply it to your own task and criteria before you publish them.
